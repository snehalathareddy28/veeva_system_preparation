1. Paper: EHR-RAG
Main idea
=>The paper proposes EHR-RAG, a retrieval-augmented framework for reasoning over long-horizon Electronic Health Records (EHRs).
The problem is that a patient's medical history can contain many visits, events, laboratory results and records.
=> A normal LLM may not be able to effectively use all of this information because of context-window limitations and incomplete retrieval.
=>The paper therefore introduces:
*Event- and time-aware retrieval
*Adaptive iterative retrieval
*Dual-path factual and counterfactual reasoning
*Evidence retrieval for long patient histories 
2601.21340v1.pdf
=> Main result :
*The experiments showed that EHR-RAG consistently improved performance over LLM-based baselines. 
It reported an average 10.76% Macro-F1 improvement across the evaluated long-horizon clinical prediction tasks.
2601.21340v1.pdf
=>Paper's conclusion:
*The authors conclude that carefully designed retrieval and reasoning mechanisms allow LLMs to work more effectively with longitudinal structured EHR data, 
even when the model has strict context limitations. 
*They position EHR-RAG as a step toward more reliable, data-efficient and evidence-grounded clinical AI.
2601.21340v1.pdf
*Importantly, the authors also state that their work is a research contribution rather than a deployed clinical system and does not make autonomous treatment recommendations. 
They highlight privacy, fairness and appropriate use of AI-assisted decision support as important considerations.
